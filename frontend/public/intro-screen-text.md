## Physician Introduction

```
Dr. Elias Pemberton, Literary Physician
Curing souls with words since the Victorian era

I've been practicing literary medicine for 178 years. In that time,
I've learned three truths: suffering is universal, literature is
eternal, and modern humans are remarkably unoriginal in their misery.

Whatever you're feeling has been felt before, written better, and
survived by someone cleverer than you. That's not comforting. It's
just true.

No pills. No platitudes. No positive thinking.
Just British literature and a healthy dose of cynicism.
```

## Service Description

```
What I offer: Literary prescriptions for modern ailments

You tell me what's wrong with your soul.
I prescribe a passage from British literature.
You decide whether to take the medicine or remain miserable.

The choice, as always, is yours.
```

## Tone Variations for Different Moods

### Sympathetic Opening
```
I understand you're struggling.

So was Virginia Woolf. So was Sylvia Plath.
So was half the Bloomsbury Group.

They wrote about it. Some survived it.
All of them understood it better than your therapist does.

Let's see what they have to say about your particular variety of suffering.
```

### Sardonic Opening
```
Another soul in crisis. How refreshing.

Let me guess: you're feeling lost, anxious, misunderstood,
and vaguely certain that previous generations had it easier.

They didn't. They just had better writers.

Shall we see what Dickens has to say about your problems?
He had twelve children and died of a stroke at 58.
Perspective is therapeutic.
```

### Direct Opening
```
State your complaint.

I have 2,000 years of British literature at my disposal
and 178 years of experience matching misery to prose.

Your suffering is not unique. Your need for it to be unique
is also not unique. I've seen this before.

The prescription will either help or it won't.
Both outcomes are instructive.

Choose your affliction below.
```

## Visual Atmosphere Descriptions

### Background Elements
```
- Subtle parchment texture
- Faded ink spots in corners
- Victorian wallpaper pattern (very low opacity)
- Aged paper borders around text blocks
- Sepia-toned divider lines (not harsh gold)
```

### Interactive Elements
```
Emotion Tag Buttons:
- Typewriter-style text
- Subtle hover effect (slight glow, not garish)
- Click sound (optional): vintage typewriter key
- Border style: dotted/dashed (medical prescription aesthetic)
```

### Typography Hierarchy
```
Main Title: Playfair Display, 72px, Bold, Gold (#C5A059)
Subtitle: Courier Prime, 14px, Regular, Cream (#F5F5DC)
Body Text: Courier Prime, 16px, Regular, Cream (#F5F5DC)
Quotes: Playfair Display, 24px, Italic, Cream (#F5F5DC)
Dividers: ASCII art style (━━━━━━), Gold (#C5A059)
```

## Accessibility Notes

```
Despite the Dark Academia aesthetic, ensure:
- Text contrast meets WCAG AA standards (cream on charcoal passes)
- Button states are clearly differentiated
- Focus indicators visible for keyboard navigation
- Screen reader friendly alt text for decorative elements
```

## Mobile Considerations

```
On smaller screens:
- Reduce intro text length (keep core message)
- Stack emotion tags 2x4 instead of 4x2
- Simplify decorative borders
- Maintain font sizes for readability
- Consider collapsible "About Dr. Pemberton" section
```
